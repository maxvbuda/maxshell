## Fractions
aka: fraction, how fractions work, adding fractions
A fraction is part of a whole: 3/4 means 3 parts out of 4 equal parts. The top number is the numerator, the bottom the denominator. To add fractions, give them the same denominator first: 1/2 + 1/4 = 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4.

To multiply fractions, multiply the tops and the bottoms: 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2.
Q: What is half of a quarter? | One eighth (1/8).

## Percentages
aka: percent, percentage, how to calculate a percentage
Percent means "out of 100", so 25% is 25/100, a quarter. To find a percentage of a number, multiply by the percentage and divide by 100: 20% of 50 is 50 × 20 / 100 = 10.

To work out what percentage one number is of another, divide and multiply by 100: 15 out of 60 is 15 / 60 × 100 = 25%.

## Pi
aka: pi, π, what is pi
Pi (π) is the ratio of a circle’s circumference to its diameter — the same for every circle, about 3.14159. Its digits go on forever without repeating. It’s used everywhere circles appear: a circle’s circumference is π × diameter and its area is π × radius².

## The Pythagorean theorem
aka: pythagoras, pythagorean theorem, right triangle
In a right-angled triangle, the square of the longest side (the hypotenuse) equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides: a² + b² = c². So a triangle with sides 3 and 4 has a hypotenuse of 5, because 9 + 16 = 25.

It’s named after the ancient Greek mathematician Pythagoras and is used in building, navigation and computer graphics.

## Algebra
aka: algebra, solving equations, what is x in maths
Algebra uses letters like x to stand for unknown numbers, so you can write rules and solve puzzles. To solve an equation, do the same thing to both sides until x is alone: if 2x + 3 = 11, subtract 3 to get 2x = 8, then divide by 2 to get x = 4.

## Prime numbers
aka: prime number, primes
A prime number is a whole number bigger than 1 that can only be divided exactly by 1 and itself: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 and so on. 2 is the only even prime. Every whole number above 1 is either prime or a product of primes — 12 = 2 × 2 × 3.

Primes are the basis of the encryption that keeps the internet secure.
Q: Is 1 a prime number? | No — a prime needs exactly two different divisors, and 1 only has one.

## Negative numbers
aka: negative number, minus numbers
Negative numbers are less than zero, like −5. They’re used for temperatures below zero, money owed and positions below sea level. Adding a negative is like subtracting, and multiplying two negatives gives a positive: −3 × −4 = 12.

## Music
aka: how music works, music theory basics, notes and chords
Music is sound organised in time: rhythm (the pattern of beats), melody (a tune of notes one after another) and harmony (notes played together, called chords). Western music uses 12 notes that repeat in higher and lower octaves; a major scale sounds bright and a minor one sadder.

People make music with their voices and with instruments that use strings, air, or things you hit — and now with computers too.

## Painting and drawing
aka: how to draw, drawing tips, painting, art
Drawing starts with looking closely: break things into simple shapes, then refine. Light and shadow make things look 3D, and perspective makes far things smaller. Practice beats talent — sketch a little every day.

Painters work in oils, acrylics or watercolours; famous artists include Leonardo da Vinci, Vincent van Gogh, Frida Kahlo and Pablo Picasso.

## Poetry
aka: poem, poems, how to write a poem, what is a poem
A poem uses words for their sound and feeling as well as their meaning. Poems can rhyme or not, and often use rhythm, repetition and imagery — pictures in words. A haiku is a tiny Japanese form with three lines of 5, 7 and 5 syllables; a limerick is a funny five-line poem.

To write one, pick something you feel strongly about, notice small details, and read it aloud to hear how it sounds.

## Stories
aka: how to write a story, writing a story, creative writing
A good story has a character who wants something, a problem in the way, and a change by the end. Start close to the action, show what characters do and say rather than just telling us how they feel, and give the reader small, specific details.

Write a messy first draft, then improve it — every writer rewrites.

## Football
aka: soccer, football, how football works
Football (soccer in the US) is the world’s most popular sport. Two teams of eleven try to kick the ball into the other team’s goal; only the goalkeepers can use their hands. A match lasts 90 minutes in two halves. The World Cup, held every four years, is the biggest tournament.

## Basketball
aka: basketball, how basketball works, nba
In basketball, two teams of five score by shooting the ball through a hoop 10 feet (3.05 m) high. A basket is worth 2 points, or 3 from behind the three-point line, and free throws are worth 1. Players move the ball by dribbling or passing. It was invented by James Naismith in 1891.

## The Olympic Games
aka: olympics, olympic games
The modern Olympic Games began in Athens in 1896, inspired by the ancient Greek games. The Summer and Winter Olympics are each held every four years, with athletes from over 200 countries competing in sports from swimming and athletics to skiing and skating. Winners get gold, silver and bronze medals.

## Chess
aka: chess, how to play chess
Chess is a strategy game for two players on an 8×8 board. Each side has a king, a queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights and eight pawns, and each piece moves differently. The goal is checkmate: attacking the opponent’s king so it can’t escape.

Good beginner tips: control the centre, develop your knights and bishops early, and keep your king safe by castling.

## Languages
aka: languages of the world, learning a language, how to learn a language
About 7,000 languages are spoken in the world. The most spoken, counting second-language speakers, are English, Mandarin Chinese, Hindi and Spanish. To learn one, practise a little every day, learn the most common words first, listen to real speakers, and don’t be afraid of mistakes — speaking early helps most.

## Grammar basics
aka: grammar, parts of speech, nouns and verbs
Words have jobs. Nouns name things (dog, city, idea), verbs are actions or states (run, is), adjectives describe nouns (red, happy) and adverbs describe verbs (quickly). A sentence needs a subject and a verb: "The dog ran."

Common mix-ups: "your" (belonging to you) and "you’re" (you are); "its" (belonging to it) and "it’s" (it is); "there", "their" and "they’re".
Q: What is the difference between your and you're? | "Your" means belonging to you (your book); "you’re" is short for "you are" (you’re late).

## Movies
aka: films, cinema, how movies are made
Movies are made by large teams: a writer’s script, a director who shapes the story, actors, cinematographers who film it, and editors who cut it together, plus music, sound and visual effects. The first films appeared in the 1890s; sound arrived in the late 1920s and colour soon after.

Animated films are drawn, modelled with computers, or made with stop-motion puppets.
