## The Earth
aka: earth, planet earth
Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only place we know of with life. It is about 4.5 billion years old and about 12,700 km wide. Around 71% of its surface is covered by water, mostly oceans. Its atmosphere is mostly nitrogen (78%) and oxygen (21%), and it protects us from the Sun’s harmful rays.

Inside, Earth has layers: a thin rocky crust, a thick hot mantle, a liquid outer core and a solid inner core about as hot as the Sun’s surface. Its one natural satellite is the Moon.
Q: How old is the Earth? | About 4.5 billion years.
Q: How much of the Earth is covered by water? | About 71%.

## Continents
aka: the seven continents, how many continents
There are seven continents: Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe and Australia (sometimes called Oceania). Asia is the largest and most populated; Australia is the smallest. Antarctica is the coldest and has no permanent residents, only scientists at research stations.

The continents were once joined in a supercontinent called Pangaea, about 300 million years ago, and have slowly drifted apart ever since.
Q: What is the largest continent? | Asia, both by area and by population.
Q: What was Pangaea? | A supercontinent, about 300 million years ago, when all the continents were joined together.

## Oceans
aka: the five oceans, ocean
There are five oceans: the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern and Arctic. The Pacific is the largest and deepest — bigger than all the land on Earth combined. Its Mariana Trench plunges nearly 11,000 metres, deeper than Everest is tall.

Oceans produce much of the world’s oxygen (from tiny plankton), absorb heat and carbon dioxide, and are home to a huge variety of life, much of it still unexplored.
Q: What is the largest ocean? | The Pacific Ocean.
Q: What is the deepest point in the ocean? | The Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench, nearly 11,000 metres down.

## Mount Everest
aka: everest, the tallest mountain
Mount Everest, on the border of Nepal and China (Tibet), is the highest mountain above sea level at 8,849 metres. It was first climbed in 1953 by Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay. The air at the top holds only about a third of the oxygen at sea level, so most climbers carry bottled oxygen.

Everest is still growing a few millimetres a year, as the Indian tectonic plate pushes into Asia.
Q: How tall is Mount Everest? | 8,849 metres (29,032 feet).
Q: Who first climbed Everest? | Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay, in 1953.

## Rivers
aka: longest river, the nile, the amazon
The Nile in Africa and the Amazon in South America are the two longest rivers, each over 6,400 km; which is longer depends on how you measure. The Amazon carries by far the most water — about a fifth of all the river water flowing into the world’s oceans.

Rivers have shaped history: many early civilisations grew along them, like Egypt on the Nile and Mesopotamia between the Tigris and Euphrates, because rivers bring water, fertile soil and transport.
Q: What is the longest river in the world? | The Nile or the Amazon, depending on how they’re measured — both are over 6,400 km.

## Deserts
aka: desert, sahara
A desert is a place that gets very little rain — less than about 25 cm a year. Deserts can be hot, like the Sahara in Africa, the largest hot desert, or cold: Antarctica is technically the largest desert of all.

Desert plants and animals have clever adaptations: cacti store water in their stems, and camels can go days without drinking.
Q: What is the largest desert? | Antarctica, if you count cold deserts; the Sahara is the largest hot desert.

## Rainforests
aka: rainforest, amazon rainforest
Tropical rainforests are warm, wet forests near the equator. Though they cover only about 6% of the land, they’re home to over half of the world’s plant and animal species. The Amazon is the largest, spreading across nine countries in South America.

Rainforests store huge amounts of carbon and help produce rain. Many medicines come from rainforest plants. They’re threatened by logging, farming and fires.

## Weather and climate
aka: weather, climate
Weather is what the atmosphere is doing right now — sunny, rainy, windy. Climate is the average weather of a place over many years. So “it’s raining today” is weather; “this region has wet winters” is climate.

Weather is driven by the Sun heating the Earth unevenly, which creates wind, and by water moving through the water cycle. Meteorologists forecast weather using satellites, weather stations and computer models.
Q: What is the difference between weather and climate? | Weather is day-to-day conditions; climate is the long-term average for a place.

## Countries and population
aka: how many countries, world population
There are 195 countries in the world: 193 members of the United Nations plus two observer states, the Vatican City and Palestine. Russia is the largest by area; Vatican City is the smallest. The world’s population passed 8 billion people in 2022.

India and China are the most populous countries, each with over 1.4 billion people. More than half of the world’s people now live in cities.
Q: How many countries are there? | 195 — 193 United Nations members plus two observer states.
Q: What is the largest country? | Russia, by area.
Q: What is the most populated country? | India, which passed China in 2023; both have over 1.4 billion people.

## Time zones
aka: time zone
Because the Earth rotates, it’s daytime in some places and night in others, so the world is split into time zones. There are roughly 24, each about one hour apart, measured from Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), based on the Prime Meridian in Greenwich, London.

Some countries use half-hour offsets, and many change their clocks for daylight saving time in summer. China uses a single time zone despite being wide enough for five.
