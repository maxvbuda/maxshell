## Sleep
aka: how much sleep do i need, why we sleep, sleeping well
Sleep lets your body and brain recover: memories are sorted and stored, the body repairs itself, and you wake up able to focus. Adults need about 7–9 hours a night; teenagers about 8–10; children even more.

To sleep better, keep a regular bedtime, avoid screens and caffeine late in the day, and keep your room dark, quiet and cool.
Q: How much sleep do teenagers need? | About 8 to 10 hours a night.

## Exercise
aka: staying fit, working out, why exercise is good
Exercise strengthens your heart, muscles and bones, improves your mood and helps you sleep. Health guidelines suggest at least an hour of activity a day for young people and around 150 minutes a week of moderate exercise for adults, plus some strength exercises.

It doesn’t have to be a sport: walking, cycling, dancing and playing outside all count.

## Healthy eating
aka: eating healthy, a balanced diet, nutrition
A balanced diet includes plenty of vegetables and fruit, whole grains, protein (like beans, fish, eggs or meat), some dairy or alternatives, and water. Foods high in sugar, salt and fat are fine sometimes but shouldn’t be most of what you eat.

Eating a variety of colours on your plate is an easy way to get many different vitamins.
Q: How much water should I drink? | Roughly 6 to 8 glasses a day, more when it’s hot or you’re active. Pale yellow urine is a good sign you’re drinking enough.

## Saving money
aka: how to save money, budgeting, a budget
Saving starts with knowing where your money goes. Make a simple budget: write down what comes in and what goes out, then decide how much to save before you spend. Small things add up — a little saved every week becomes a lot over a year.

Set a goal, keep savings somewhere separate from spending money, and wait a day before buying things you don’t need.
Q: What is interest? | Money paid for using money: a bank pays you interest on savings, and you pay interest when you borrow.

## Making friends
aka: how to make friends, friendship, being a good friend
Friendships grow from spending time together. Join clubs or activities you enjoy, ask people questions about themselves and listen to the answers, and say yes when they invite you places. Being kind, reliable and yourself matters more than being cool.

Good friends support each other, keep promises and can disagree without falling out.

## Studying
aka: how to study, study tips, revising for exams, how to study for a test
Study in short sessions with breaks (for example 25 minutes on, 5 off), test yourself instead of just re-reading, and spread revision over several days rather than cramming the night before. Explaining a topic out loud, as if teaching it, shows you what you really understand.

Sleep matters too: your brain stores what you learned while you sleep.

## Cooking basics
aka: how to cook, learning to cook, cooking for beginners
Start with simple recipes: eggs, pasta, rice, a stir-fry or soup. Read the whole recipe first, get everything ready before you start, and taste as you go. Wash your hands and keep raw meat separate from other food.

Salt, a little acid like lemon juice, and not overcooking vegetables make a big difference to flavour.
Q: How do I boil an egg? | Put it in boiling water: about 6–7 minutes for a soft yolk, 10–12 for hard-boiled. Then cool it in cold water.
Q: How do I cook pasta? | Boil plenty of salted water, add the pasta, stir, and cook for the time on the packet (usually 8–12 minutes) until it’s tender but still a little firm. Drain and add sauce.
Q: How do I cook rice? | Rinse 1 cup of rice, add 2 cups of water and a pinch of salt, bring to the boil, then cover and simmer on low for about 15–18 minutes until the water is absorbed. Let it rest 5 minutes.

## Stress
aka: dealing with stress, feeling stressed, anxiety tips, how to calm down
Stress is your body’s reaction to pressure. A little helps you focus; too much makes you tired, worried and tense. Things that help: slow breathing (in for 4, out for 6), a walk or exercise, enough sleep, breaking big tasks into small steps, and talking to someone you trust.

If worry or sadness lasts a long time or feels like too much, talk to a parent, teacher, doctor or counsellor — that’s a smart thing to do, not a weak one.

## Time management
aka: being productive, how to be productive, procrastination, how to stop procrastinating
Write down what you need to do, pick the most important thing, and start with a small step — even five minutes. Put your phone away while you work, and do hard tasks when you have the most energy.

Breaking work into chunks with short breaks makes it feel less overwhelming, and finishing small pieces builds momentum.

## Weather
aka: how weather works, why it rains, clouds
Weather is the state of the air around us: temperature, wind, clouds and rain. The Sun heats the Earth unevenly, warm air rises and cools, and the water vapour in it condenses into clouds. When cloud droplets join and get heavy enough, they fall as rain — or snow when it’s cold.

Weather forecasts use measurements from all over the world and computer models of the atmosphere.
Q: Why is it colder on mountains? | Air gets thinner higher up and expands as it rises, which cools it — so mountain tops are colder.

## First aid
aka: basic first aid, what to do in an emergency
In an emergency, first make sure you’re safe, then call for help — the emergency number (911 in the US, 112 in Europe, 999 in the UK). For a small cut, clean it with water and cover it with a plaster; for a burn, cool it under running water for 20 minutes.

Learning a first-aid course is one of the most useful skills there is.

## Pets
aka: having a pet, looking after a pet, getting a dog, getting a cat
Pets need daily care: food, fresh water, a clean place to live, exercise, attention and visits to the vet. Dogs need walks and training and love company; cats are more independent but still need play and affection; small pets like hamsters and fish need the right cage or tank kept clean.

Before getting a pet, think about how much time, space and money it needs for its whole life.
