## Ancient Egypt
aka: egyptians, the pyramids, pharaohs
Ancient Egypt was a civilisation along the Nile River in north-east Africa that lasted for about 3,000 years, from around 3100 BCE. Its rulers, the pharaohs, were seen as gods on Earth. The Nile’s yearly floods made the land fertile, allowing farming in the middle of the desert.

The Egyptians built the pyramids as tombs — the Great Pyramid of Giza, built around 2560 BCE, was the tallest structure in the world for almost 4,000 years. They wrote with hieroglyphs, made paper from papyrus, and mummified their dead to prepare them for the afterlife.
Q: Why were the pyramids built? | As tombs for pharaohs, to protect their bodies and treasures for the afterlife.
Q: How old are the pyramids? | The Great Pyramid of Giza is about 4,500 years old, built around 2560 BCE.

## Ancient Greece
aka: greeks, athens, sparta
Ancient Greece was a group of city-states, like Athens and Sparta, that flourished from about 800 to 146 BCE. Athens developed the first democracy, where citizens voted on laws. Greek thinkers such as Socrates, Plato and Aristotle founded Western philosophy, and Greeks made great advances in mathematics, science, theatre and architecture.

The first Olympic Games were held in Olympia in 776 BCE. Greek myths about gods like Zeus and heroes like Hercules are still told today.
Q: Where did democracy start? | In ancient Athens, around 500 BCE.
Q: When were the first Olympic Games? | In 776 BCE, in Olympia, Greece.

## Ancient Rome
aka: romans, roman empire
Rome grew from a small town in Italy into an empire that ruled much of Europe, North Africa and the Middle East. It was a republic for centuries, then became an empire in 27 BCE under Augustus. At its height, around 117 CE, it had tens of millions of people.

Romans built roads, aqueducts to carry water, concrete buildings like the Colosseum, and a system of law that still influences many countries. Latin, their language, grew into Italian, Spanish, French, Portuguese and Romanian. The Western Roman Empire fell in 476 CE; the Eastern (Byzantine) Empire lasted until 1453.
Q: When did the Roman Empire fall? | The Western Roman Empire fell in 476 CE; the Eastern part lasted until 1453.
Q: Who was the first Roman emperor? | Augustus, in 27 BCE.

## The Middle Ages
aka: medieval times, middle ages, knights and castles
The Middle Ages, or medieval period, is the time in Europe from the fall of Rome (476 CE) to about 1500. Society was organised by feudalism: kings granted land to lords, who were protected by knights, while most people were peasants working the land. The Church was very powerful.

It was the age of castles, knights and cathedrals, but also of terrible plagues — the Black Death killed about a third of Europe’s people in the 1340s. Meanwhile, the Islamic world was a centre of science and learning, and great empires rose in China, Africa and the Americas.
Q: What was the Black Death? | A plague that swept through Europe and Asia in the 1340s and killed about a third of Europe’s population.

## The Renaissance
aka: renaissance
The Renaissance (“rebirth”) was a period of renewed interest in art, science and learning in Europe, from about the 1300s to the 1600s, starting in Italy. Artists like Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo and Raphael created masterpieces; scientists like Galileo studied the natural world by observing and experimenting.

Johannes Gutenberg’s printing press, around 1440, made books cheap and spread ideas faster than ever before.
Q: Who invented the printing press? | Johannes Gutenberg, around 1440.

## The Age of Exploration
aka: explorers, columbus
From the 1400s to the 1600s, European sailors explored the world’s oceans looking for trade routes. Christopher Columbus reached the Americas in 1492; Vasco da Gama sailed around Africa to India in 1498; and Ferdinand Magellan’s expedition was the first to sail around the world (1519–1522).

These voyages connected continents, but also led to the colonisation of the Americas, Africa and Asia, the devastation of Indigenous peoples by war and disease, and the Atlantic slave trade.
Q: When did Columbus reach the Americas? | In 1492.

## The Industrial Revolution
aka: industrial revolution
The Industrial Revolution began in Britain in the late 1700s and spread around the world. Machines powered by water and then steam replaced hand work, factories grew, and people moved from farms to cities. Inventions like the steam engine, the spinning jenny and railways changed how goods were made and moved.

It made goods cheaper and eventually raised living standards, but early factory work was dangerous, with long hours and child labour, and cities became crowded and polluted.
Q: Where did the Industrial Revolution start? | In Britain, in the late 1700s.

## World War I
aka: first world war, ww1, the great war
World War I lasted from 1914 to 1918. It began after the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary and grew into a war between the Allies (including Britain, France, Russia and later the USA) and the Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary and the Ottoman Empire).

Much of it was fought in trenches, with terrible losses: around 20 million people died. It ended with an armistice on 11 November 1918, and reshaped the map of Europe and the Middle East.
Q: When was World War I? | From 1914 to 1918.

## World War II
aka: second world war, ww2
World War II, from 1939 to 1945, was the deadliest conflict in history, with around 70–85 million deaths. It began when Nazi Germany, led by Adolf Hitler, invaded Poland. The Allies — including Britain, the Soviet Union, the USA and China — fought the Axis powers of Germany, Italy and Japan.

During the Holocaust, the Nazis murdered six million Jewish people and millions of others. The war ended in Europe in May 1945 and in the Pacific in September 1945, after the USA dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Afterwards, the United Nations was founded to help keep peace.
Q: When did World War II end? | In 1945 — May in Europe and September in the Pacific.
Q: When did World War II start? | In 1939, when Germany invaded Poland.

## The Cold War
aka: cold war
The Cold War (about 1947–1991) was a long period of tension between the United States and its allies and the Soviet Union and its allies. They never fought each other directly, but competed through an arms race, the space race, and wars in other countries. The Berlin Wall divided East and West Berlin from 1961 until it fell in 1989.

The Cold War ended when the Soviet Union broke apart in 1991.
Q: When did the Berlin Wall fall? | On 9 November 1989.

## The space race
aka: space race, moon landing
The space race was a competition between the USA and the Soviet Union to achieve firsts in space. The Soviets launched the first satellite, Sputnik, in 1957, and sent the first person into space, Yuri Gagarin, in 1961. The USA landed the first people on the Moon, with Apollo 11 in July 1969.
Q: When did people first land on the Moon? | On 20 July 1969, with Apollo 11.

## The civil rights movement
aka: civil rights, martin luther king
The American civil rights movement of the 1950s and 1960s fought to end racial segregation and discrimination against Black Americans. Leaders like Martin Luther King Jr., Rosa Parks and many others used peaceful protests, boycotts and marches. King’s “I Have a Dream” speech in 1963 is one of the most famous in history.

The movement led to landmark laws like the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965.
Q: Who was Rosa Parks? | A civil rights activist who, in 1955, refused to give up her bus seat to a white passenger, sparking the Montgomery bus boycott.

## The internet's history
aka: history of the internet, who invented the internet
The internet grew from ARPANET, a US research network that sent its first message in 1969. In the 1970s, Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn designed TCP/IP, the rules that let different networks connect. In 1989, Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web — websites, links and browsers — which made the internet easy for everyone to use.

Today, more than 5 billion people are online.
Q: Who invented the World Wide Web? | Tim Berners-Lee, in 1989.
Q: Is the internet the same as the web? | No — the internet is the global network; the web is the websites and links that run on top of it.
