## Albert Einstein
aka: einstein
Albert Einstein (1879–1955) was a German-born physicist, one of the most important scientists ever. His theory of relativity changed our understanding of space, time and gravity, and his equation E = mc² shows that mass and energy are two forms of the same thing. He won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921 for explaining the photoelectric effect, which helped found quantum physics.

He moved to the United States in 1933 to escape the Nazis. His name has become a byword for genius.
Q: What does E = mc² mean? | Energy equals mass times the speed of light squared — a small amount of mass holds an enormous amount of energy.

## Isaac Newton
aka: newton
Isaac Newton (1643–1727) was an English mathematician and physicist. He described the laws of motion and universal gravitation, showed that white light is made of all the colours of the rainbow, and — at the same time as Gottfried Leibniz — invented calculus. His book “Principia” (1687) is one of the most influential science books ever written.

## Marie Curie
aka: curie
Marie Curie (1867–1934) was a Polish-French physicist and chemist who pioneered research on radioactivity — a word she helped coin. She discovered the elements polonium and radium. She was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize, and is still the only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences: physics (1903) and chemistry (1911).

## Charles Darwin
aka: darwin
Charles Darwin (1809–1882) was an English naturalist who developed the theory of evolution by natural selection. On a five-year voyage aboard HMS Beagle he observed animals like the finches of the Galápagos Islands, which helped him see how species adapt. His book “On the Origin of Species” (1859) transformed biology.

## Leonardo da Vinci
aka: da vinci, leonardo
Leonardo da Vinci (1452–1519) was an Italian painter, inventor and scientist of the Renaissance. He painted the Mona Lisa and The Last Supper, and filled notebooks with designs for flying machines, studies of anatomy, and observations of nature, written in mirror writing. He’s often called the ultimate “Renaissance man” because of how many fields he mastered.

## William Shakespeare
aka: shakespeare
William Shakespeare (1564–1616) was an English playwright and poet, widely seen as the greatest writer in the English language. He wrote around 39 plays, including Romeo and Juliet, Hamlet, Macbeth and A Midsummer Night’s Dream, and 154 sonnets. He invented or popularised many words and phrases we still use, like “break the ice”.

## Ada Lovelace
aka: lovelace
Ada Lovelace (1815–1852) was an English mathematician often called the first computer programmer. She worked with Charles Babbage on his Analytical Engine, a mechanical computer design, and wrote what’s considered the first algorithm meant for a machine. She also saw that computers could do more than arithmetic — for example, compose music.

## Alan Turing
aka: turing
Alan Turing (1912–1954) was an English mathematician and a founder of computer science. He described the “Turing machine”, a simple model of how any computer works, and during World War II he helped break the German Enigma code, shortening the war. He also proposed the Turing test for machine intelligence. He was persecuted for being gay; the UK formally apologised in 2009.

## Grace Hopper
aka: hopper
Grace Hopper (1906–1992) was an American computer scientist and US Navy rear admiral. She created one of the first compilers — programs that translate human-readable code into machine code — and helped develop COBOL, one of the first widely used programming languages. She popularised the word “debugging” after a real moth was found stuck in a computer.

## Nelson Mandela
aka: mandela
Nelson Mandela (1918–2013) was a South African leader who fought against apartheid, the system of racial segregation. He spent 27 years in prison for his activism. After his release in 1990 he worked for peaceful change, and in 1994 became South Africa’s first Black president, elected in the country’s first fully democratic election. He won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1993.

## Martin Luther King Jr.
aka: mlk, martin luther king
Martin Luther King Jr. (1929–1968) was an American minister and leader of the civil rights movement. He championed non-violent protest against racial segregation and discrimination, leading the Montgomery bus boycott and the 1963 March on Washington, where he gave his “I Have a Dream” speech. He won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1964 and was assassinated in 1968.

## Mahatma Gandhi
aka: gandhi
Mohandas “Mahatma” Gandhi (1869–1948) led India’s movement for independence from British rule using non-violent resistance, like the 1930 Salt March. His ideas inspired civil rights movements around the world. India became independent in 1947.

## Abraham Lincoln
aka: lincoln
Abraham Lincoln (1809–1865) was the 16th president of the United States. He led the country through the Civil War and issued the Emancipation Proclamation in 1863, which declared enslaved people in the Confederate states free; slavery was abolished nationwide by the 13th Amendment in 1865. He was assassinated in 1865.

## Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart
aka: mozart
Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (1756–1791) was an Austrian composer and child prodigy who started composing at five. In his short life he wrote more than 600 works, including symphonies, concertos and operas such as The Magic Flute and The Marriage of Figaro. He’s one of the most performed classical composers.

## Galileo Galilei
aka: galileo
Galileo Galilei (1564–1642) was an Italian astronomer and physicist. He improved the telescope and used it to discover Jupiter’s four largest moons, the phases of Venus and the craters on the Moon — evidence that Earth goes around the Sun. His support for this idea put him in conflict with the Church, and he spent his last years under house arrest.
